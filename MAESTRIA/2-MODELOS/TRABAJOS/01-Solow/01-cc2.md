
# With technology as a simple $Ae^{\varepsilon}$ process

$y_{t}=A_{t}k_{t}h_{t}^{1-\theta}$

Given that it is a random walk, we try solving it in differences:
$$
\gamma = \frac{k_{t+1}}{k_{t}}
$$
Movement:
$$
\gamma _{t}= \frac{1-\delta}{1+\lambda}  +  \frac{sA_{t} h_{t}^{1-\theta} }{1+\lambda} 
$$

Steady State:
$$
\bar{\gamma} = \frac{1-\delta}{1+\lambda}  +  \frac{s\bar{A} \bar{h}^{1-\theta} }{1+\lambda} 
$$
Define:
$$
\tilde{\gamma}_{t+1} = \ln \left( \frac{\gamma}{\bar{\gamma}} \right)
$$
Therefore:
$$
\bar{\gamma} (1+\tilde{\gamma}_{t+1})= \frac{1-\delta}{1+\lambda}  +  \frac{s \bar{A} (1+\varepsilon _{t}) \bar{h}^{1-\theta} }{1+\lambda} 
$$
Subtracting the steady state value
$$
\tilde{\gamma}_{t+1} \bar{\gamma} = \frac{s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}}{1+\lambda} \varepsilon _{t}
$$
We now convert this to a path for $k_{t}$
$$
\tilde{\gamma}_{t+1}  =\frac{1}{\bar{\gamma}} \frac{s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}}{1+\lambda} \varepsilon _{t}
$$
$$
\ln(\gamma) - \ln(\bar{\gamma})  =\frac{1}{\bar{\gamma}} \frac{s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}}{1+\lambda} \varepsilon _{t}
$$
$$
\ln(\gamma)   =\frac{1}{\bar{\gamma}} \frac{s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}}{1+\lambda} \varepsilon _{t} + \ln(\bar{\gamma})
$$
$$
\ln\left(  \frac{k_{t+1}}{k_{t}} \right)   =\frac{1}{\bar{\gamma}} \frac{s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}}{1+\lambda} \varepsilon _{t} + \ln(\bar{\gamma})
$$
$$
\frac{k_{t+1}}{k_{t}} =\exp \left\{ \frac{1}{\bar{\gamma}} \frac{s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}}{1+\lambda} \varepsilon _{t} + \ln(\bar{\gamma}) \right\} 
$$
$$
k_{t+1} =\exp \left\{ \frac{1}{\bar{\gamma}} \frac{s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}}{1+\lambda} \varepsilon _{t} + \ln(\bar{\gamma}) \right\} \cdot k_{t}

$$
The capital accumulation process reveals several fundamental economic relationships through its parametric structure. Current capital ($k_t$) enters multiplicatively, preserving existing stock levels while transmitting growth effects forward. The savings rate ($s$) and labor input ($\bar{h}$) jointly determine investment efficiency, with their positive influence scaled by the labor share parameter ($1-\theta$). Population growth ($\lambda$) appears inversely, capturing the dilution effect that partitions capital across more firms. The steady-state growth rate ($\bar{\gamma}$) exhibits dual roles: its logarithm drives the baseline growth trend, while its reciprocal modulates technology shock ($\varepsilon_t$) transmission. This multiplicative shock structure ensures capital remains strictly positive and the exponential form guarantees non-negativity, even with large deviations from steady-state behavior.
# With technology as an AR(1) process


growth rate:
$$
\gamma_t = \frac{k_{t+1}}{k_{t}}
$$

The growth rate equation becomes:
$$
\gamma_t = \frac{1-\delta}{1+\lambda} + \frac{sA_t k_th^{1-\theta}}{1+\lambda}
$$

Where technology follows:
$$
A_t = \rho A_{t-1} + (1-\rho)\bar{A} + \varepsilon_t
$$

Steady State (when $A_t = \bar{A}$):
$$
\bar{\gamma} = \frac{1-\delta}{1+\lambda} + \frac{s \bar{A}  \bar{k} h^{1-\theta}}{1+\lambda}
$$
Define:
$$
\tilde{\gamma}_{t+1} = \ln \left( \frac{\gamma}{\bar{\gamma}} \right)
$$
Therefore:
$$
\bar{\gamma} (1+\tilde{\gamma}_{t+1})= \frac{1-\delta}{1+\lambda}  +  \frac{s [\rho A_{t-1} + (1-\rho)\bar{A} + \varepsilon_t] \bar{h}^{1-\theta} }{1+\lambda} 
$$

$$
\bar{\gamma} (1+\tilde{\gamma}_{t+1})= \frac{1-\delta}{1+\lambda}  +  \frac{ [\rho A_{t-1}s\bar{h}^{1-\theta} + (1-\rho)\bar{A} s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}+ s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}\varepsilon_t]  }{1+\lambda} 
$$
$$
\bar{\gamma} (1+\tilde{\gamma}_{t+1})= \frac{1-\delta}{1+\lambda}  +  \frac{ \rho A_{t-1}s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}  }{1+\lambda} +\frac{(1-\rho)\bar{A}s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}}{1+\lambda}+ \frac{s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}\varepsilon_t}{1+\lambda}
$$
Subtracting the steady state value
$$
\bar{\gamma} \tilde{\gamma}_{t+1}= \frac{1-\delta}{1+\lambda}  +  \frac{ \rho A_{t-1}s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}  }{1+\lambda} +\frac{(1-\rho)\bar{A}s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}}{1+\lambda}+ \frac{s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}\varepsilon_t}{1+\lambda} - \frac{1-\delta}{1+\lambda} - \frac{s \bar{A}  \bar{k} h^{1-\theta}}{1+\lambda}
$$
$$
\bar{\gamma} \tilde{\gamma}_{t+1}=   \frac{ \rho A_{t-1}s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}  }{1+\lambda} +\frac{(1-\rho)\bar{A}s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}}{1+\lambda}+ \frac{s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}\varepsilon_t}{1+\lambda} - \frac{s \bar{A}  \bar{k} h^{1-\theta}}{1+\lambda}
$$
$$
\bar{\gamma} \tilde{\gamma}_{t+1}=   \frac{ \rho A_{t-1}s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}  }{1+\lambda} -\frac{\rho\bar{A}s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}}{1+\lambda}+ \frac{s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}\varepsilon_t}{1+\lambda} 
$$
$$
\bar{\gamma} \tilde{\gamma}_{t+1}=   \frac{ \rho s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}  }{1+\lambda} (A_{t-1} - \bar{A})  + \frac{s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}}{1+\lambda}  \varepsilon_t
$$

We now convert this to a path for $k_{t}$
$$
\tilde{\gamma}_{t+1}  =\frac{1}{\bar{\gamma}} \left(  \frac{ \rho s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}  }{1+\lambda} (A_{t-1} - \bar{A})  + \frac{s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}}{1+\lambda}  \varepsilon_t \right) 
$$
$$
\ln(\gamma) - \ln(\bar{\gamma})  =\frac{1}{\bar{\gamma}} \left(  \frac{ \rho s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}  }{1+\lambda} (A_{t-1} - \bar{A})  + \frac{s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}}{1+\lambda}  \varepsilon_t \right) 
$$
$$
\ln(\gamma)   =\frac{1}{\bar{\gamma}} \left(  \frac{ \rho s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}  }{1+\lambda} (A_{t-1} - \bar{A})  + \frac{s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}}{1+\lambda}  \varepsilon_t \right) + \ln(\bar{\gamma})
$$
$$
\ln\left(  \frac{k_{t+1}}{k_{t}} \right)   =\frac{1}{\bar{\gamma}} \left(  \frac{ \rho s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}  }{1+\lambda} (A_{t-1} - \bar{A})  + \frac{s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}}{1+\lambda}  \varepsilon_t \right) + \ln(\bar{\gamma})
$$
$$
\frac{k_{t+1}}{k_{t}} =\exp \left\{\frac{1}{\bar{\gamma}} \left(  \frac{ \rho s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}  }{1+\lambda} (A_{t-1} - \bar{A})  + \frac{s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}}{1+\lambda}  \varepsilon_t \right) + \ln(\bar{\gamma})\right\} 
$$
$$
k_{t+1} =\exp \left\{ \frac{1}{\bar{\gamma}} \left(  \frac{ \rho s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}  }{1+\lambda} (A_{t-1} - \bar{A})  + \frac{s\bar{h}^{1-\theta}}{1+\lambda}  \varepsilon_t \right) + \ln(\bar{\gamma}) \right\} \cdot k_{t}
$$
Here it won't be a random walk if 
$$
\varepsilon _{t} > \rho (\bar{A} - A_{t-1})
$$
