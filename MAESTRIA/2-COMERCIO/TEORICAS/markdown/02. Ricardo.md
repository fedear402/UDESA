## Ricardian model: 2 countries, 2 goods

### Basic ingredients:
- two countries (foreign (*) and home), two goods ($i = 1, 2$)
- Only one factor of production: labor. $L$, $L^*$: total amount of labor in the country.
- Technology: Production function of goods in countries are linear in labor: $Y_i = \frac{l_i}{a_i}$ and $Y_i^*(l_i^*) = \frac{l_i^*}{a_i^*}$
- $a_i$ is the labor needed per unit of production of good $i$ at home country. So $MPL = 1/a_i$.
- $a_i^*$ is the labor needed per unit of production of good $i$ at foreign country. So productivity is $MPL^* = 1/a_i^*$.
- Assume labor is perfectly mobile between industries in a given country, but totally immobile across countries (no migration).
→ This implies that both goods are produced in the home country only if the wages earned in the two industries are the same
- Assume same utility function and homotetic preferences in both countries.

## Ricardo 2x2 Model

### Home
The production functions for the two goods in the home country are:

$$Y_1 = \frac{1}{a_1}l_1$$

$$Y_2 = \frac{1}{a_2}l_2$$

where $l_1$ and $l_2$ are the labor inputs for goods 1 and 2 respectively, and $L$ is the total labor force:

$$l_1 + l_2 = L$$

Frontera de posibilidades de produccion (FPP) of home country:

$$Y_2 = \frac{L}{a_2} - \frac{a_1}{a_2}Y_1$$

## Autarky Equilibrium

From the maximization of producers, we need If the country is producing both goods, then Wages in each sector is the marginal productivity of labor.

$$w_i = p_i MPL_i = \frac{p_i}{a_i} \quad \forall i = 1, 2 \quad (1)$$

In autarky, wages across sectors are equalized (Why?):

$$\frac{p_1^A}{a_1} = \frac{p_2^A}{a_2} = w \Rightarrow \frac{p_1^A}{p_2^A} = \frac{MPL_2^A}{MPL_1}$$

where $p_1^A$ and $p_2^A$ are the autarky prices for goods 1 and 2. Note that this is the same as:

Autarky relative prices in the home country:
$$\frac{p_1^A}{p_2^A} = \frac{a_1}{a_2}$$

Similarly, for the foreign country:
$$\frac{p_1^{*A}}{p_2^{*A}} = \frac{a_1^*}{a_2^*}$$

Note that in autarky, relative prices are entirely pinned down by technology (supply side).

---

## Comparative Advantage

**Absolute Advantage (AA)**: A country is more productive in producing a good.  
**Comparative Advantage (CA)**: An economy's ability to produce a particular good at a lower opportunity cost.

Assume the home country has a comparative advantage in producing good 1. By definition, this implies that the opportunity cost for the home country of producing good 1 in terms of good 2 is smaller than that of the foreign country.

$$\frac{a_1^*}{a_2^*} > \frac{a_1}{a_2}$$

Recall that here $MPL_i = 1/a_i$ so this is equivalent to say that foreign is relative more efficient in producing good 2.

$$\frac{MPL_2^*}{MPL_1^*} > \frac{MPL_2}{MPL_1}$$

In Autarky, comparative advantage of country 1 in good 1 implies then:

$$\frac{p_1^A}{p_2^A} < \frac{p_1^{*A}}{p_2^{*A}}$$

---

## Trade Prices and Specialization

Lets assume that demand is such that trade relative prices satisfy:

$$\frac{p_1^A}{p_2^A} < \frac{p_1}{p_2} < \frac{p_1^{*A}}{p_2^{*A}}$$

Note that this implies that:
$$\frac{p_1}{p_2} > \frac{a_1}{a_2}$$

**Claim**: Home country will fully specialize in the good in which has comparative advantage (good 1).

**Workers perspective** Relative wages in the country are:

$$\frac{w_1}{w_2} = \frac{\frac{p_1}{a_1}}{\frac{p_2}{a_2}} = \frac{p_1}{p_2} \frac{a_2}{a_1} = \frac{p_1/p_2}{p_1^A/p_2^A} > 1$$

But we know that $\frac{p_1}{p_2} > \frac{p_1^A}{p_2^A}$ so workers have incentives to work in sector 1. They will all move there!

**Intuition**: with a higher relative price in the industry of good 1, workers can move there and gain higher wages. Similarly, in the foreign country, all workers will want to work in sector 2. Both countries will specialize and their will be gains from trade.

---

## Gains from Trade:

In general, one would need to see what happens with utility. But a necessary condition is that real expenditure increased. We will show that at the new equilibrium, home can consume more.

To see this, consider home country:

**Claim**: With the new wage, workers can buy the same quantities of good 1 as before:

$$\frac{w}{p_1} = \frac{w^A}{p_1^A}$$

**Proof**: At home, we know that $w = \frac{p_1}{a_1}$. We want to show that $\frac{w}{p_1} = \frac{w^A}{p_1^A}$

$$\frac{w}{p_1} = \frac{1}{a_1} \text{ and } \frac{w^A}{p_1^A} = \frac{\frac{p_1^A}{a_1}}{p_1^A}$$

Replacing, we are done.

---

## Gains from Trade (continued):

**Claim 2**: with new wage, they can buy more quantities of good 2.

For good 2:
$$\frac{w}{p_2} = \frac{p_1}{a_1} \frac{1}{p_2} \geq \frac{w^A}{p_2^A} = \frac{p_1^A}{p_2^A} \frac{1}{a_1}$$

So, higher potential consumption of good 2 if:

$$\frac{w}{p_2} \geq \frac{w^A}{p_2^A} \text{ if } \frac{p_1}{p_2} > \frac{p_1^A}{p_2^A}$$

which we know that it holds.

What ultimately lead to gains from trade here? Real wages in terms of good 1 do not change. This is because the productivity of workers in that sector did not change, so we must have the same real wage in terms of that good. But workers gain because good 2 became cheaper than before because they can buy it from efficient producers at foreign.

## Dornbush, Fisher and Samuelson (DFS) model: Ricardo with N goods

---

## Environment: general

- One factor: Labor
- Continuum of goods: DFS innovation, before we only had 2x2 models
- Market structure: perfect competition in all market
- Two countries, home (H) and foreign (F) with different technologies
- Countries differ in size (not crucial assumption): population in H and F is $L$, $L^*$
- Homothetic preferences (Cobb-Douglas)

---

## Environment: technology

- $n$ goods. We can rank them to get diminishing H comparative advantage

$$\frac{a_1^*}{a_1} > \ldots > \frac{a_i^*}{a_i} > \ldots > \frac{a_n^*}{a_n}$$

where $a$ is home unit labor requirement and $a^*$ is foreign
- $1, 2, 3, \ldots, n$ are the indexes (names) of the goods
- home is relatively more efficient in goods with low index (e.g. 1, 2, 3)

Set $A(z) = \frac{a^*(z)}{a(z)}$

- **Hp. 1**: $A(z)$ continuous and monotonically decreasing in index $z$
- **Hp. 2**: $0 < z < 1$, there is continuum of goods (numbers) in this interval
- By construction Set $A'(z) < 0$
- $A(z)$ is a measure of H relative productivity

---

## Environment: technology (comparative advantage)

**Definition**: Home has comparative advantage (CA) in any good $z$ if

$$wa(z) \leq w^*a^*(z)$$

(recall: in perfect competition price=marginal cost, $p(z) = wa(z)$) or

$$\omega \leq A(z) \quad (CA)$$

where $\omega = w/w^*$

→ H has CA in $z$ if its relative wage is lower than its relative productivity

**Foreign has comparative advantage** in $z$ if
$$\omega \geq A(z)$$

**Borderline commodity**: $A(z) = \omega$

### Remark 1 (Comparative advantage)
Relative price combines relative wages and productivity: country can produce at a lower price if rel. wage advantage offsets rel. technology disadvantage

---

## Demand

- Tastes are identical btw. countries
- Cobb-Douglas preferences
- Expenditure share for good $z$ is

$$b(z) = \frac{p(z)c(z)}{Y} = b^*(z)$$

where
$$\int_0^1 b(z)dz = 1$$

---

## Demand ctd

For a given relative wage $\omega$, comparative advantage implies:
- goods $(0, \bar{z})$ produced by H, $(\bar{z}, 1)$ by F

Let
$$\theta(z) = \int_0^z b(z)dz > 0$$

be share of income spent on H products (goods in which H has CA)

And
$$\theta^*(z) = 1 - \theta(z) = \int_z^1 b(z)dz > 0$$

is share of income spent on F products (goods in which F has CA)

---

## Equilibrium

Different ways to close to model

$$\underbrace{wL}_{\text{Home income}} = \theta(z) \underbrace{(wL + w^*L^*)}_{\text{spending on Home goods}} \quad (E1)$$

or as trade balance

$$\underbrace{(1 - \theta(z)) wL}_{\text{H import}} = \underbrace{\theta(z)w^*L^*}_{\text{H export}} \quad (E2)$$

rearranging either one of these we get

$$\omega = \frac{\theta(z)}{1 - \theta(z)} \frac{L^*}{L} = B(z, L^*/L) \quad (B)$$

**Intuition 1**: increase in $L^*$ creates a trade surplus at home → need to compensate with ↑ $\omega$

**Intuition 2**: increase labor supply abroad (due to high population) $L^*$ and increase labor demand at home (to produce goods to export) → need to compensate with ↑ $\omega$

(CA) allows model closure: **Equilibrium when**:
$$\omega = A(\bar{z}) = B(z, L^*/L)$$

## Gains from trade

Real H consumption in autarky of good $z$

$$c(z) = \frac{b(z)wL}{p(z)} = \frac{b(z)L}{a(z)}$$

since in perfect competition $p(z) = wa(z)$.

Real H consumption in free trade

$$c(z) = \begin{cases} 
\frac{b(z)L}{a(z)} & \text{if } z < \bar{z} \\
\frac{\omega b(z)L}{a^*(z)} & \text{if } z > \bar{z}
\end{cases}$$

since $\omega > A(z)$ for $z > \bar{z}$ it follows $\frac{\omega b(z)}{a^*(z)} > \frac{b(z)}{a(z)} \Rightarrow$ H benefits from trade (similar proof for F)

### Remark 2 (intuition)
for $z > \bar{z}$ relative H wages are higher than relative H productivity → F charges lower price

## Comparative statics: Large F

Integration with large F country (e.g. China) →↑ $L^*$

Figure 1 shows ↑ $L^*$ ⇒↑ $\omega$ and ↓ $z$

**Intuition**: ↑ $L^*$ creates a trade surplus in H, as F demand has increased. To compensate H wages must go up ↑ $\omega$

**Winners and losers**: choose H labor as numeraire, $w = 1$

H is better off:
$$c(z) = \begin{cases} 
\frac{b(z)L}{a(z)} & \text{if } z < \bar{z} \\
\frac{\omega b(z)L}{a^*(z)} & \text{if } z > \bar{z}
\end{cases}$$

$\omega$ ↑ real consumption increases. Let's dig deeper

a. For goods $z$ whose production remains in H: no change in $p(z)$
b. For goods $z$ whose production remains in F: $\omega$ ↑ $\Rightarrow$ $w^*$ ↓ $\Rightarrow$ $p(z) = w^*a^*(z)$ ↓
c. For goods $z$ whose production moves to F: $w^*a^*(z) \leq a(z) \Rightarrow p(z)$ ↓

**Remark 1**. H per-capita income is $w = 1$, if real income increases, real consumption increases so we are better off. $1/p(z)$ increases for all $z$'s in case (b) and (c) above, and it's constant for case (a) $\Rightarrow$ H gains from trading with a larger country!

## Comparative statics: Foreign uniform technical change

Loss of H CA due to reduction in F cost $\Rightarrow$ loss of some industries for H and a trade deficit $\Rightarrow$ $\omega$ ↓ to compensate

Another way to see it: the improvement in technology expands F comparative advantage to a larger set of sectors ($\bar{z}$ declines), $\Rightarrow$ ↑ labor demand in F (relative to H), $\Rightarrow$ ↑ F wage (relative to H)

From (GFT): $\frac{\omega b(z)}{a^*(z)} > \frac{b(z)}{a(z)} \rightarrow \omega > A(z)$ but since $|\Delta\omega| < |\Delta A| \rightarrow$ H gains from trade¹

**Digging deeper**:
a. For goods $z$ whose production remains in H: no change in $p(z)$
b. For goods $z$ whose production remains in F: $\omega$ ↓ $\Rightarrow$ $w^*$ ↑ but since $|\Delta\omega| < |\Delta A| \Rightarrow p(z) = w^*a^*(z)$ ↓
c. For goods $z$ whose production moves to F: again $|\Delta\omega| < |\Delta A| \Rightarrow w^*a^*(z) \leq a(z) \Rightarrow p(z)$ ↓

**Remark** Since H income is still $w = 1$, real income increases as the cost reduction due to technical change more than compensate the increase in $w^*$

F is better off. Prove it at home using the logic above.

¹ $|\Delta\omega|$, $|\Delta A|$ are the changes in $\omega$ and $A$ in absolute value.

## Non-uniform foreign technical change

Assume $A_2$ declines: F technical change in **head-to-head** sectors (firms from both countries operate internationally)

F technical change can hurt H as it reduces its terms of trade: F goods in other sectors become more expensive

No gains for H in $z \in (\alpha_1, \alpha_1 + \alpha_2)$ as $|\Delta\omega| = |\Delta A|$, losses in in $z \in (\alpha_1 + \alpha_2, 1)$ as $|\Delta\omega| > |\Delta A| = 0$

## Trade costs

**Iceberg trade costs**: shipping 1 unit of good, only $0 < g < 1$ arrives to destination

H will produce $z$ iff $p_{HH} = a(z)w \leq p_{FH} = (a^*(z)/g) w^*$

$$\omega \leq \frac{A(z)}{g} = \frac{a^*(z)}{g} \frac{1}{a(z)}$$

$p_{ij}$ is price from source $i$ sold to destination $j$

F will produce $z$ iff $p_{FF} = a^*(z) w^* \leq p_{HF} = (a(z)/g) w$

$$\omega \geq A(z)g = a^*(z) \frac{1}{a(z)/g}$$

These are the conditions at which producing locally is convenient

## Trade costs

H has CA in $z$ iff $p_{HF} = \frac{a(z)}{g} w \leq p_{FF} = a^*(z) w^*$

$$\omega \leq A(z)g$$

which happens for $z < \bar{z}^*$

F will produce $z$ iff $p_{HH} = a(z)w \geq p_{FH} = \frac{a^*(z)}{g} w^*$

$$\omega \geq \frac{A(z)}{g}$$

which happens for $z > \bar{z}$

between $\bar{z}^*$ and $\bar{z}$ each country produces and sells only locally

Trade liberalization (an increase in $g$) brings the $A(z)g$ and the $A(z)/g$ curves closer together → reducing set of non-traded goods → increasing trade

---

## Equilibrium

Balanced trade condition

$$\underbrace{(1-\theta(\bar{z})) wL}_{\text{H import}} = \underbrace{(1-\tilde{\theta}(z^*)) w^*L^*}_{\text{H export}}$$

which yields

$$\omega = \frac{1-\tilde{\theta}(z^*)}{1-\theta(\bar{z})} \frac{L^*}{L} \quad (2)$$

where

$$\theta(\bar{z}) = \int_0^{\bar{z}} b(z)dz > 0$$

and

$$\tilde{\theta}(z^*) = \int_1^{z^*} b(z)dz > 0$$

---

## Equilibrium

From comparative advantage definition we have

$$A(\bar{z}) = \omega g \text{ and } A(z^*) = \omega/g \quad (3)$$

these two equation plus trade balance (2) gives equilibrium $\bar{z}$, $z^*$ and $\omega$

**Proof**: note that $\bar{z} = A^{-1}(\omega g)$ and $z^* = A^{-1}(\omega/g)$ are both decreasing in $\omega$, so plug into (2)⁴

$$\omega = \frac{1 - \tilde{\theta}(A^{-1}(\omega/g))}{1 - \theta(A^{-1}(\omega g))} \frac{L^*}{L}$$

since $\tilde{\theta}(A^{-1}(\omega/g))$ is increasing and $\theta(A^{-1}(\omega g))$ is decreasing in $\omega$, there is unique solution (see figure next slide)

$$\bar{\omega} = \bar{\omega}\left(\frac{L^*}{L}, g\right)$$

once we have $\bar{\omega}$, (3) gives $\bar{z}$ and $z^*$

⁴ $A^{-1}$ is the inverse function of $A$ in implicit form. Example in explicit form: if $A(\bar{z}) = \bar{z}^{-2}$, $z = A^{-1}(\omega g) = (\omega g)^{-1/2}$
