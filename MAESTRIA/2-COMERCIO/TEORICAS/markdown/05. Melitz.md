## Motivation

Firm-level data revolution in the 1990s highlighted new facts

**Cross-sectional facts**
- Fact 1. Only a **small fraction** of firms export;
- Fact 2. Exporters sell most of their output **domestically**;
- Fact 3. Exporters are **bigger** than non-exporters;
- Fact 4. Exporters are **more productive** than non-exporters.

**Trade liberalization facts**
- Fact 5. Trade liberalization reduces firm' probability of surviving, pushing less productive firms out (**Selection I**)
- Fact 6: trade liberalization increases the share of exporting firms (**Selection II**)
- Fact 7. Selection $\Rightarrow$ trade liberalization increases aggregate productivity

## Extensive margin of export

- an increase in trade costs reduces probability of firms entering the export market
- the effect is weaker for more productive firms:
- this is the typical selection or extensive margin of trade:
  - lower barriers to trade implies that more firms export
  - more productive firms select into the export market (their export participation is less sensitive to trade costs)

## Summing up

- The most productive firms **self-select into export markets**
- Substantial **reallocation effects within an industry** following trade liberalization.
  - Exposure to trade forces firms to **exit** (especially the least productive)
  - Exposure to trade facilitates firms' **entering into export markets**
  - Trade liberalization leads to market share **reallocation** towards more productive firms, thereby increasing **aggregate productivity**
- Trade increases productivity at the sector level
  - The sectoral improvement come from a **between effect** (selection/reallocation)

## From the data to the model

These studies suggest that a successful theoretical framework should include two features:

1. Within-sector **heterogeneity in size and productivity**
2. A feature that leads only more productive firms to export: **fixed or sunk costs** of exporting

**Contributions:**

## Environment

**Preferences.** CES preferences over a continuum of varieties $\omega \in (0, M)$

$$U = \left( \int_0^M q(\omega)^{\rho} d\omega \right)^{\frac{1}{\rho}} \quad \text{(Utility)}$$

with $0 < \rho < 1$ and $\sigma = 1/(1 - \rho) > 1$ elasticity of substitution between varieties

**Technology**
$$l(q) = f + \frac{q}{\varphi} \quad \text{(technology)}$$

- $l(q)$: labor needed to produce $q$ units of differentiated good
- technology **heterogeneous** across varieties (only difference wrt. Krugman, 1980)
- Increasing returns to scale modeled as fixed cost $f$

---

## Households

Households choose consumption of each variety $q(\varphi)$ to max

$$\max U = \left( \int_0^M q(\omega)^{\frac{\sigma-1}{\sigma}} d\omega \right)^{\frac{\sigma}{\sigma-1}}$$

$$s.t. \int_0^M p(\omega)q(\omega)d\omega = wL$$

which yields

$$q(\omega) = \left( \frac{p(\omega)}{P} \right)^{-\sigma} \frac{wL}{P} \quad (1)$$

$P$ is the price of the bundle of CES goods (price index)

$$P = \left( \int_0^M p(\omega)^{1-\sigma} d\omega \right)^{\frac{1}{1-\sigma}}$$

---

## Production

Firms problem is

$$\max_{p(\omega)} \pi(\omega) = p(\omega)q(\omega) - w \left( f + \frac{q(\omega)}{\varphi} \right)$$

$$s.t. \quad q(\omega) = \left( \frac{p(\omega)}{P} \right)^{-\sigma} \frac{wL}{P}$$

leads to markup pricing:

$$p(\omega) \equiv p(\varphi) = \frac{\sigma}{\sigma - 1} \frac{w}{\varphi} \quad (2)$$

wage is the numeraire $w \equiv 1$ and by assumption $L \equiv 1$

---

## Production

Using the optimal price (2) and the demand function we get the optimal profit function

$$\pi(\omega) = \pi(\varphi) = \left( \frac{1}{\sigma} \right) \left( \frac{1}{P} \right)^{1-\sigma} \left( \frac{\sigma}{\sigma - 1} \right)^{1-\sigma} \varphi^{\sigma-1} - f = B\varphi^{\sigma-1} - f$$

where $B$ is aggregate demand shifter equal for all firms

**Monopolistic competition**: each variety produced by 1 firm, so can replace variety symbol $\omega$ with productivity $\varphi$

---

## Survival cutoff

- A entry firms pay fixed cost $f_e$ to draw productivity from a given distribution and earn profits: $\pi(\varphi) = B\varphi^{\sigma-1} - f$
- If profit $\pi(\varphi) > 0$, firm produce, otherwise they exit
- Let $\varphi^*$ be lowest productivity firm that is just indifferent between producing and exiting

**Zero Profit Condition (ZPC):**
$$B(\varphi^*)^{\sigma-1} = f \quad \text{(ZPC)}$$

Firms with $\varphi > \varphi^*$ have positive profits

$$\frac{\pi(\varphi) + f}{\pi(\varphi^*) + f} = \left( \frac{\varphi}{\varphi^*} \right)^{\sigma-1}$$

$$\pi(\varphi) = \left[ \left( \frac{\varphi}{\varphi^*} \right)^{\sigma-1} - 1 \right] f$$

→ ratio trick: express profits as function of firm productivity $\varphi$ and the cutoff (useful for solution)

---

## Entry

- Firms pay a cost $f_e$ to enter the market (draw a productivity $\varphi$)
- **Free entry**: expected profits driven to zero
- Firms don't know their productivity before paying the entry cost, so they expect to earn: $E_{\varphi>\varphi^*} \left[ \left( \frac{\varphi}{\varphi^*} \right)^{\sigma-1} - 1 \right] f$

**Free entry implies**

$$E_{\varphi>\varphi^*} \left[ \left( \frac{\varphi}{\varphi^*} \right)^{\sigma-1} - 1 \right] f = f_e \quad \text{(FE)}$$

**Equilibrium**: ZPC and FE determine $\varphi^*$, $B$

## Open economy: basics

- **Two identical countries**
- Backbone trade model is Krugman (1980) so **Gains from Trade (GFT) through Variety**
- **Plus new GFT**: selection of most productive firms increases productivity
- After entry firms decide which market to sell
  - for domestic sales pay fixed cost $f$
  - for export pay fixed cost $f_x$ (e.g. foreign distributors, adapt product to foreign standards and regulations, understand shipping rules)
- Export also involves iceberg transportation costs $\tau > 1$

---

## Production

A firm with productivity $\varphi$ solves

$$\max_{q_d,q_x} \Pi(\varphi) = \left[ p_d - \varphi^{-1} \right] q_d L - f + \left[ p_x - \tau\varphi^{-1} \right] q_x L^F - f_x$$

$$s.t. \quad q_d(\varphi) = \left( \frac{p_d(\varphi)}{P} \right)^{-\sigma} \frac{wL}{P}$$

$$q_x(\varphi) = \left( \frac{p_x(\varphi)}{P^F} \right)^{-\sigma} \frac{w^F L^F}{P^F}$$

$\pi_d, \pi_x$: domestic profits, export profits, $F$ indicates the foreign country

Since countries are symmetric firms face same demand and size $L = L^F \equiv 1$

Since the countries are symmetric, $w = w^F$, $L = L^F$, and $P = P^F$

---

## Production

The firm problem is then identical to Krugman (1980)

Solving the export and domestic sales problem separately as in Krugman we get the usual markup pricing

$$p_d(\varphi) = \frac{\sigma}{\sigma - 1} \frac{1}{\varphi}, \quad p_x(\varphi) = \frac{\sigma}{\sigma - 1} \frac{\tau}{\varphi}$$

---

## Decision to export

If $\pi_d \geq 0$ sell to the domestic market, so **domestic cutoff** is
$$B(\varphi^*)^{\sigma-1} = f \quad (3)$$

If $\pi_x \geq 0$ sell to the foreign market, so **export cutoff** is
$$B(\varphi_x^*/\tau)^{\sigma-1} = f_x \quad (4)$$

Note: due to country symmetry the demand shifters are the same, $B = B^F$

**Cutoff ratio**: divide (3) and (4)
$$(\varphi_x^*/\varphi^*)^{\sigma-1} = \tau^{\sigma-1} f_x/f \quad (5)$$

Exporters are more productive than non exporters (it's easier to sell domestically) if
$$\tau^{\sigma-1} f_x > f$$

## Equilibrium in open economy

**ZPC**: $B(\varphi^*)^{\sigma-1} = f$ and $B(\varphi_x^*/\tau)^{\sigma-1} = f_x$

Profit from domestic sales for firm $\varphi$ is (using ratio trick)
$$\pi_d(\varphi) = \left[ \left( \frac{\varphi}{\varphi^*} \right)^{\sigma-1} - 1 \right] f$$

Profit from export sales for firm $\varphi$ is
$$\pi_x(\varphi) = \left[ \left( \frac{\varphi}{\varphi_x^*} \right)^{\sigma-1} - 1 \right] f_x$$

**Free entry condition FE:**
$$E_{\varphi \geq \varphi^*} \left[ \left( \frac{\varphi}{\varphi^*} \right)^{\sigma-1} - 1 \right] f + E_{\varphi \geq \varphi_x^*} \left[ \left( \frac{\varphi}{\varphi_x^*} \right)^{\sigma-1} - 1 \right] f_x = f_e$$

## Trade liberalization

Reduction in trade costs makes foreign firms more competitive → domestic market demand falls so non-exporters lose market shares

**Least productive domestic firms exit**, selection effect I: **exit margin** ($\varphi^*$ rises)

**Exporting is cheaper** so:
**Most productive non exporters start exporting**, selection effect II: **new exporters margin** ($\varphi_x^*$ declines)

Each of these responses reallocates resources towards higher productivity firms generating an increase in **average industry productivity**. (we'll see this next)

## Gains from trade (GFT)

Assume productivity at entry is Pareto with shape $\kappa > 1$ and scale $\varphi_{min}$: the density function (PDF) is

$$f(\varphi) = k\varphi_{min}^{\kappa}/\varphi^{\kappa+1}$$

and cumulative distribution function (CDF) is

$$F(\varphi) = 1 - \left( \frac{\varphi_{min}}{\varphi} \right)^{\kappa}$$

Average productivity at entry is: (no need to derive it, believe me)

$$E(\varphi) = \frac{\kappa}{\kappa - 1} \varphi_{min}$$

## Gains from trade (GFT)

As Krugman (1980), welfare is proportional to the price index, which is a negative function of average productivity

**Home welfare is**
$$W = \frac{1}{P} = \frac{1}{P(\tau, \bar{\varphi})}$$

$P$ is a function of average productivity

$$P = \left( \int_0^M p(\omega)^{1-\sigma} d\omega \right)^{\frac{1}{1-\sigma}} = E_{\varphi \geq \varphi^*} \left( \frac{\sigma}{(\sigma-1)\varphi} \right)^{\sigma-1} + E_{\varphi \geq \varphi_x^*} \left( \frac{\sigma\tau}{(\sigma-1)\varphi} \right)^{\sigma-1}$$

Hence, the selection effect by increasing average productivity generates a new channel of gains from trade

**GFT 1**: ($\downarrow \tau$) increases $W$ via reduction in import prices (love for variety) (Krugman 1980)

**GFT 2**: New source of GFT via the **selection effect** on average productivity (Melitz, 2003)

## Winners and losers

Easier to see with simpler exercise of moving from **Autarky to Trade**

Domestic market demand changes from its autarky level $B^A$ to $B$.

$B$ cannot be higher than $B^A$: from definition of $B$

$$B = \frac{1}{\sigma} \left( \frac{\sigma}{\sigma - 1} \right)^{1-\sigma} P^{\sigma-1}$$

due to GFT 1 and GFT 2 the price index under trade must be lower than under autarky → $B^A > B$

This implies that total profit curves $\pi(\varphi)$ and $\pi^A(\varphi)$ must intersect (see Figure)

## Winners and losers

**Compared to autarky:**
- All non-exporting firms lose profits, some of them lose all and exit
- Least productive exporters lose profits
- Only the **most productive exporters** gain profits
- Since productivity distribution is highly skewed, there are only few firms at the top $\Rightarrow$ **gains are highly concentrated!**

**Unequal gains from trade:**
- **all citizens gain as consumers**, trade leads to lower prices (GFT1 and GFT2 above)
- **most citizens lose as entrepreneurs**, their firms shut down or lose profits
- **a few citizens**, those owning top firms, **gain in profits**

**Remark**

*Extensions of the basic model which allow for unemployment show that workers in 'losing' firms will be unemployed or experience a pay cut, even when the effect on aggregate unemployment is negative.*

## Taking stock

- Firm level data show that models without firm heterogeneity face a number of empirical challenges.
- The Melitz (2003) model of heterogenous firms and trade explains:
  - That only few firms in an industry export.
  - Selection of the most productive firms into export markets.
  - Increases in average industry productivity following trade liberalization: **new GFT**
- The main insight is that trade liberalization would make it more difficult for high-cost firms to survive and market shares will be allocated towards the low-cost firms
- **Corollary**: winning and losing firms from trade
